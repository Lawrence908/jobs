The Sahm rule: the recession trigger

Percentage points. The three-month average unemployment rate minus its low over the prior year. Readings at or above 0.50 have marked the start of every US recession since 1970. The US line is Sahm's real-time series; the Canadian line borrows the arithmetic on revised data, an analogue rather than the validated rule. Currently: US …, Canada ….

Jobs growth, year over year

Percent change from a year earlier. The two measures are deliberately different animals: US payrolls count jobs from an employer survey, Canadian employment counts people from a household survey. Growth below zero outside a shaded band has been rare for a reason.

Sources and methods

The modern series

  • United States: Bureau of Labor Statistics, via FRED. Unemployment rate UNRATE (monthly since 1948), nonfarm payrolls PAYEMS (1939), participation CIVPART (1948), initial claims ICSA and continued claims CCSA (weekly, 1967), the Sahm rule SAHMREALTIME (1959), JOLTS openings JTSJOL and quits JTSQUR (December 2000). All seasonally adjusted. Fetched from the keyless CSV endpoint first, the keyed API as fallback.
  • Canada: Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey, table 14-10-0287-01, vectors v2062815 (unemployment rate), v2062811 (employment), v2062816 (participation), 15 and over, seasonally adjusted, monthly since January 1976. EI regular-benefit recipients from table 14-10-0011-01, vector v64549350, since 1997. Fetched keyless from the Web Data Service; every vector's live title is verified against the published table before its numbers are trusted, because a guessed vector produces a plausible wrong number.

The historical extensions

  • US 1929-1947: annual averages from the Census Bureau's Historical Statistics of the United States, Series D85-86, transcribed from the printed table (p.135). Persons 14 and over; 1929-1939 are Lebergott's reconstructions, the CPS household survey begins in March 1940. Drawn dashed and labelled estimate.
  • The dispute is content: Series D85-86 counts federal relief workers (WPA and kin) as unemployed. Counting them as employed, Darby (1976) puts 1933 at 20.6% against the … shown, and 1941 at 6.6% against 9.9%. The gap is definitional, not arithmetic: whether a relief job was a job. Both readings are honest; this page draws the Census series and says so.
  • Canada 1921-1945: June-of-each-year estimates derived from Historical Statistics of Canada, Series D124-133: persons without jobs and seeking work over the civilian labour force. Census benchmarks (1921, 1931, 1941) extrapolated with trade-union and employer reports by the Dominion Bureau of Statistics; 14 and over; excludes Newfoundland, which joined Confederation in 1949. June readings, not annual averages, and drawn as point-to-point estimates.
  • Canada 1946-1975: Labour Force Survey annual averages from the same volume, Series D223-235, on the original 14-and-over basis (quarterly survey to 1952, monthly after). The post-1976 revised basis reads one to three tenths lower where they overlap. The monthly line starts in 1976 on the current basis; the seam is visible and labelled, not smoothed over.

Recession shading

  • US bands derive mechanically from NBER dates via FRED USREC: peak month through trough month, … recessions since 1854 in the shared dataset, drawn here from 1921 on.
  • Canadian bands are the C.D. Howe Institute Business Cycle Council chronology (Commentary 366, Table 1), thirteen recessions from April 1929, COVID-19 dated by the Council's 2021 declaration. No API exists; the dates are curated with citations and change roughly once per cycle.

Reading the two countries against each other

  • The headline rates are close cousins, not twins: the US counts 16 and over, Canada 15 and over, and survey definitions differ at the edges (Canada counts passive job search, the US does not). Statistics Canada publishes a US-concept adjustment that runs roughly a point below the headline Canadian rate. The gap on this page is real but its width is partly definitional.
  • US payrolls count jobs at establishments; Canadian employment counts employed people at households. A person with two jobs is two payrolls but one employed person.
  • Every series here is seasonally adjusted by its publisher.

Plumbing

  • Data refreshes daily via cron; monthly surveys move once a month and weekly claims once a week, so most runs verify rather than change. The header stamp says when data last moved.
  • Series follow the shared econ-core contract (id, provenance, units, frequency, confidence, observations, splices), the same shape as the rest of the collection at debt and diesel, so the eventual overlay can put a claims spike against a credit spread without refactoring.
  • The schema reserves a vintages field for as-published (ALFRED) views: what a reader saw in October 2008, not what the revised history now says. Designed in now, populated later.
  • Raw data: /api/data, or the files under /data/.
